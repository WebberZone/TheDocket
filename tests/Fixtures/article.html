Why slow reading matters

By Jane Writer

Most of what we read online is skimmed. We open a tab, glance at the first paragraph, and move on, telling ourselves we will come back to it later. The tabs pile up, the reading list grows, and very little of it is ever read with any care.

Slow reading is the opposite habit. It means choosing fewer things and giving each one your full attention, the way you might read a good novel on a long train journey. It is not about reading slowly for its own sake but about reading in a way that lets ideas settle.

A read-later list helps, but only if it is a place you return to. Saving something is a promise to your future self, and the list is only useful when that promise is kept. Archive what you have finished and delete what no longer interests you.

There is also the question of where the reading happens. A clean page, a comfortable typeface and no adverts make a surprising difference to how long you stay with a piece of writing. This link should lose its href. Further reading is still allowed.

Finally, keep what matters. Links rot and sites disappear, so a reading habit worth having is one that keeps its own copy of the things you valued enough to finish. Docket stores the text and images of each article for exactly that reason.
